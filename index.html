A domain is the only ad you buy once.

Every other channel is rented. You stop paying, it stops working, and the month you spent building it evaporates.

A keyword domain doesn't behave that way. When your address is the phrase your customer searched, the name works in the search result, in the AI answer, on the truck, on the invoice and out of somebody's mouth at a barbecue. It keeps doing that whether or not you funded it this month.

That's the whole argument, and it's worth being honest about its limit: a domain is not a ranking. Google has said plainly that an exact-match name is not a shortcut to the top of the results. What the name actually buys you is clarity — somebody reads it and knows in under a second what you do and where you do it, and they remember it without trying. Clarity is what converts. Ranking is what the site you build on top of it earns.

Renting the phrase

You bid against every competitor in your market for the same click, every day, forever. The month you stop, someone else is standing where you stood. You built nothing you keep.

Owning the phrase

The name is on the title. Nobody outbids you for it. It appreciates or it doesn't, but it can't be taken away, and it sits on the books as an asset instead of on the expense sheet as a line item.
