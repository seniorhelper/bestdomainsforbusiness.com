Acquisition engagement

For a specific name held by somebody else.

engagement fee to start the work, then 15% of the purchase price on close — 15% or $1,500, whichever is greater.

  • Ownership research beyond the public record
  • Written valuation with comparable sales

The engagement fee is for the work and the report, which get delivered whether or not the name transfers. It is not a deposit against the purchase price and it is not refundable if the owner says no. Full terms on the terms page.

Why it is not free

Nobody works a negotiation for free, and you should be suspicious of anyone who offers to.

A pure contingency broker has exactly one incentive: get the deal closed, at whatever number, so they get paid. That is the opposite of what you want from the person advising you on whether the price is fair.

Charging for the work means we can tell you the name is not worth what the owner wants. It means we can recommend walking away. It means the report you get is honest rather than a sales document.

And we say this on the price card, not in fine print: most owners never reply. Many will not sell at any price. If that is how yours goes, you still get the research, the valuation and the documented attempt — which is what you paid for.

Before you engage us

Read these first.

What does the $1,500 actually buy me?

A written acquisition report and the work behind it: ownership research past the public record, a valuation with comparable sales so you know what a fair number looks like, a documented approach to the owner, and the owner’s actual answer in writing. You keep all of that whether or not the name changes hands. We are not asking you to pay for a maybe — we are charging for work that gets done either way.

And if it closes?

15% of the purchase price, or $1,500, whichever is greater. The engagement fee is separate from the commission, the same way it works at most buyer-side brokerages.

Why should I pay anything before you get a result?

Because the research and the negotiation are the work, and they happen whether the owner says yes or no. A broker working purely on contingency has one incentive: push you to overpay so the deal closes. Charging for the work removes that pressure and lets us tell you when a name is not worth what the owner wants.

Can you promise you will get the name?

No, and nobody honest will. Plenty of owners will not sell at any price. Some never reply to anyone. Some want a number that is simply unreasonable and our advice will be to walk. What we promise is the work, the report and a straight recommendation — including when that recommendation is to stop.

How long does it take?

Research is usually a few days. Owner response is the variable and it is entirely out of our hands — some answer the same week, some take a month, some never do. We report back either way rather than leaving you wondering.

What if the name has a trademark problem?

We will tell you before you spend a dollar on it. Buying a name that shadows somebody’s live mark is how people lose the name and the money together. We are not attorneys and this is not legal advice — if there is a mark in play we will tell you to get a trademark lawyer involved, and we mean it.

Do you already own something close?

Quite possibly. Our portfolio is deep and we will always check ours first, because buying from us is faster and cheaper than negotiating with a stranger. Search it yourself here.
