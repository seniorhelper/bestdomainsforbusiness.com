How to choose a domain name for a business

Three rules that have held up for fourteen years of buying, building and selling these.

1. Say the phrase, not the brand. A customer with a leaking roof types "roofing company Denver," not "Apex Summit Solutions." A name that contains the phrase is understood instantly, remembered without effort, and repeats itself every time it is spoken.

2. Get the .com. Other extensions work, but .com is the one people assume, the one 95% of consumers recognize, and the one that does not need "dot-net" explained on the phone.

3. Buy the asset, not the starting line. A developed domain with age, rankings and backlinks is worth more than a fresh registration for the same reason a house is worth more than a lot. If one exists for your trade, ask for it.

The honest caveat, again: the name is not a ranking. Google has said so plainly. The name is the foundation; the site is the building. We do both.
