Type a name — or up to five, separated by commas — and see exactly how it scores on the things that make a domain easy to say, type and remember. Every rule and every point is on this page. Nothing is sent anywhere.

The rules, in full

How the 100 points work.

We publish the scoring so you can disagree with it. These rules measure how easily a person can hear, say, type and remember a name — the things buyers and customers notice first.

RuleMaxHow points are given

What this score is not: a dollar value or a prediction of rankings. Google has said keywords in a domain do not earn a ranking on their own, and no calculator knows which buyer needs your name. For value, use our honest worth checklist.

Straight answers

Questions people ask.

Is a higher score worth more money?

Not necessarily. The score measures readability and memorability. Price depends mostly on whether a specific buyer needs that exact name. A long, specific name can be worth more to the right business than a short generic one.

Why does .com get the most points?

Because people type .com by habit and it is the largest extension by registrations, according to Verisign's quarterly Domain Name Industry Brief. Other extensions work, but some visitors and email will drift to the .com.

Does this tool store or send what I type?

No. The scoring runs in your browser. The only thing loaded is our own inventory file, so we can show similar names we own.
