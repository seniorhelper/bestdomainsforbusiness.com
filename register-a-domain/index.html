Picking well

Six rules for a name you will not regret.

Say it out loud first

If you have to spell it on the phone, it is the wrong name. That single test kills more bad domain ideas than any tool.

Skip hyphens and numerals

They do not hurt your site. They hurt every conversation where somebody has to repeat the name back to you.

Shorter beats clever

Clever needs explaining. Plain-language names do not, which is why exact-match names still sell so well.

Check the trademark

Before you register anything close to an existing brand, search the USPTO database. Registering a name that shadows a live mark is how people lose the name and pay for the privilege.

Think about the next five years

A name that locks you into one city or one service is fine if that is the plan. It is a problem if you intend to expand.

Grab the near-miss

If the plural or the common misspelling is cheap, take it and point it at the main name. It costs less than losing the traffic.

Straight answers

Registration questions.

What is the difference between registering a domain and buying one?

Registering means nobody has it yet and you are claiming it for the first time. Buying means somebody already holds it and you are acquiring it from them. Registering costs a normal annual fee. Buying costs whatever the current owner will accept. Always check whether the name is simply available first — people skip that step constantly and overpay for a variation.

Do I actually own a domain I register?

You hold the registration for as long as you keep renewing it. Nobody owns a domain outright in perpetuity — that is not how the system works for anyone, anywhere. Keep it renewed and keep the contact details current, and it stays yours.

How many should I register?

One that you will actually use, plus the obvious variants if the name is easy to get slightly wrong. Registering thirty variations of one idea is a common and expensive mistake. Registering the plural of the name that is going on your truck is usually smart.

Should I register a .com or something else?

Default to .com unless you have a reason not to. People type .com by reflex, and for a business that sells to the general public that reflex matters more than any clever extension. Research has found the extension itself does not predict whether AI engines cite you, so pick the one your customers will remember, not the one that sounds modern.
