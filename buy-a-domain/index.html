How buying works

Four steps, no theater.

  1. Tell us which name and what you are building.

    The plan changes the price. A name that is going to carry a business is a different conversation than a name you are parking, and we price it accordingly.

  2. Get a real number on the phone.

    No reserve, no auto-bid, no expiring offer. If the number does not work, say so and we will talk about it. Financing and lease-to-own are available on some names — ask.

  3. Pay, and the funds clear.

    Card or bank. Nothing transfers before payment clears, which protects both sides.

  4. Authorization code, same business day.

    You get the auth code and transfer instructions and push the name into your own registrar account. Registrars set the transfer window, typically a few days for a .com.

Questions people actually ask

Before you call.

How do I buy a domain name that someone already owns?

Three steps. Find out who holds it, reach the right person, and make an offer that does not get ignored. Our ownership lookup page walks you through the first step for free. If the owner is behind privacy protection or simply does not answer, that is where our acquisition service takes over.

Why are there no prices on the domains?

Because the honest number depends on what you are buying. The same name costs one thing bare, another thing with a site built on it, and another as part of a cluster. Publishing a single figure would be wrong in at least two of those three cases. Every price here is negotiable and quoted on a call.

How does the money actually move?

Card or bank payment. Once funds clear, you get the registrar authorization code and transfer instructions the same business day. Most .com transfers complete within a few days, and the registrars control that timing, not us.

Do I own it outright, or am I renting it?

Outright, unless you specifically ask for a financed or lease-to-own structure. A domain is registered, not owned forever — you hold the registration and renew it, the same as every domain on the internet. Once it transfers, the registration is in your account under your name.

Can I buy the name and have you build the site?

Yes, and most buyers do. That is a separate conversation with its own pricing — see domain plus website.

What if I am not sure the name is worth it?

Then say so on the call. We would rather talk you out of the wrong name than sell you one you resent in six months. If you want to think about what drives a name’s value first, this page lays it out.
