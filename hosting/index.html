A word about speed

Cheap and slow is not cheap.

Hosting is the easiest place to save forty dollars a year and lose far more than that in business. A page that takes three seconds to appear loses people before they have read a word, and it loses them silently — you never see the visitor who gave up.

It goes further than that now. Research into which pages AI answer engines cite has consistently found a large gap between fast-loading pages and slow ones. The infrastructure under your site has become part of whether anyone finds it at all.

None of which means you need enterprise hosting for a five-page site. It means you should not buy the absolute cheapest thing available and assume it is fine.

Hosting questions

The basics, answered plainly.

Do I need hosting if I already have a domain?

Yes, unless the name is only going to forward somewhere else. The domain is the address. Hosting is the building. You need both before anything shows up when somebody types your name.

What kind of hosting should I get?

For a normal small-business site, shared cPanel hosting is plenty and anything more is money you do not need to spend yet. If you are running a store with real traffic, or something custom, that is a different conversation — have it before you buy, not after.

Can I keep my domain in one place and host somewhere else?

Absolutely, and lots of people do. You point the nameservers at the host and the two work together. Keeping them in one account is simpler to manage; splitting them gives you more flexibility. Neither is wrong.

Does hosting affect SEO?

Indirectly, through speed and uptime, and those matter more than most people assume. Research into AI citations has repeatedly found that faster-loading pages get cited noticeably more often than slow ones. Cheap hosting that stalls under load is a real cost, not a saving.
