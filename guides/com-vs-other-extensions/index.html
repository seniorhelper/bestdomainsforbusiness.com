Should your business domain be a .com?

Usually, yes — but not for the reasons people assume. Here is what the extension actually changes, what it does not, and when something else is the better call.

By Zachary Tye Wennstedt, Eye To Ad MediaUpdated October 2026

On this page

  1. Why .com is still the default
  2. What Google does and does not care about
  3. When a non-.com is the right call
  4. Quick test before you decide

Why .com is still the default

.com is the largest top-level domain by registrations, and has been for decades — Verisign, which operates .com, publishes the figures every quarter in its Domain Name Industry Brief. That scale is the whole argument: people assume .com, type .com by habit, and phones and email apps autocomplete it.

The practical cost of a non-.com is leakage. If you own yourname.net and someone else owns yourname.com, a share of visitors and emails meant for you will go to them. Before buying any non-.com, look up who owns the .com.

What Google does and does not care about

Google has said that new generic extensions are treated like other generic extensions — no built-in boost and no penalty for being a .homes or a .agency rather than a .com.

Country-code extensions are different: Google treats a ccTLD as a signal that a site targets that country. Google’s documentation notes that some country codes, such as .tv and .me, are treated as generic because people use them that way. If you serve the U.S., a foreign country code you picked for its letters can send the wrong signal; check Google’s list before you commit.

Some country codes also restrict who can register them, for example by requiring a local presence. Read the registry’s rules first.

When a non-.com is the right call

  • You serve one country and its country code is what locals expect.
  • The .com is unaffordable, and you also control or can live with who owns it.
  • It is a campaign or product name where the extension is part of the brand.
  • You are a nonprofit (.org) or a tech product where .io or .ai is the norm in your field.

Nearly every name in our portfolio is a .com, for exactly these reasons. The exceptions are deliberate: allofus.one, denver-seo.ai, denverseo.io, oahurealestate.homes.

Quick test before you decide

Say the full name, extension included, to someone who has never heard it and ask them to type it. If they type .com instead, you have your answer. Our domain name checker scores the extension along with length, hyphens and the phone test, and shows every rule.

Straight answers

Questions people ask.

Is .com better for SEO?

Not by itself. Google treats generic extensions alike; the advantage of .com is human habit and less leakage to someone else's site.

Should I buy the .net and .org too?

Only if leakage or impersonation is a real risk for your business. For most small businesses, owning the .com and the most common misspelling is enough.
